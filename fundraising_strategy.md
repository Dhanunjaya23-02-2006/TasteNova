# How Much Equity Should You Give?

When raising your early rounds (Pre-Seed or Seed), the golden rule is to **not give away too much of your company early on**. 

- **Standard Equity to Give:** Generally, founders give away **10% to 20%** of their company in an early funding round. 
- **Danger Zone:** Giving away more than 25% in your first round is dangerous. You need to leave enough equity for yourself and future investors in later rounds.
- **Founder Control:** Retaining 80%+ after your first round ensures you stay motivated and in control of the company direction.

# Valuing Your Startup

Investment amount and equity dictate your company's valuation. 
*Math: Investment Amount / Equity Given = Post-Money Valuation*

<br/>

### Scenario A: The Pre-Seed Round
- **You ask for:** ₹30 Lakhs
- **Equity you offer:** 10%
- **Implied Valuation:** ₹3 Crores (Post-Money)
- *Best for: Angel Investors, Friends & Family, Micro-VCs*

---

<!-- Slide 4 -->
# Valuing Your Startup (Cont.)

### Scenario B: The Seed Round
- **You ask for:** ₹1 Crore
- **Equity you offer:** 15%
- **Implied Valuation:** ₹6.6 Crores (Post-Money)
- *Best for: Institutional Seed Funds, Prominent Angel Networks*

# How to Structure the Deal?

You don't always have to price your company immediately. Most early startups use flexible instruments:

### 1. SAFE Note (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)
- *Highly Recommended!*
- It's a quick, simple contract where an investor gives you money today, and that money converts into shares *later* during a future priced round. Very low legal fees.

---

<!-- Slide 6 -->
# How to Structure the Deal? (Cont.)

### 2. Convertible Note
- Similar to a SAFE, but acts as a loan with an interest rate until it converts into equity.

<br/>

### 3. Priced Equity Round
- You actually issue fresh shares at a fixed price immediately. Requires more legal work, valuation agreements, and negotiation.

# What You Need Prepared 

Before you talk to an investor, have a "Data Room" (a Google Drive folder) ready with:

1. **Pitch Deck:** *(Already Done!)*
2. **App/Platform Demo:** A video recording or TestFlight/APK link proving the product works. *(Huge advantage for TasteNova!)*

# What You Need Prepared (Cont.)

3. **Financial Projections (Model):** An Excel sheet showing your expected costs, revenue, and cash burn for the next 18-24 months.
4. **Cap Table:** A simple spreadsheet showing who currently owns what percentage of the company (usually just you at 100% right now).

# Legal Requirements

Investors won't wire money to your personal bank account. You must have:

- **Private Limited Company (Pvt Ltd):** You must incorporate TasteNova as a Pvt Ltd company in India. Investors cannot easily invest in Sole Proprietorships or LLPs.
- **Current Account:** A corporate bank account in the company's name.
- **Founder Vesting Agreement:** Investors will want you to earn your shares over time (usually 4 years). This proves you won't take the money and leave the startup on day 1.

# How to Find & Approach Investors

**1. Angel Networks (India)**
Apply to groups like Indian Angel Network, Mumbai Angels, or Lead Angels. They pool money from multiple rich individuals.

**2. Micro VCs & Accelerators**
Firms like 100X.VC, Y Combinator, or Blume Founders Fund are great for early-stage startups with a built MVP.

**3. Cold Outreach via LinkedIn / Twitter**
Find founders of successful food-tech startups and message them for advice. Advice often leads to investment!

# The Fundraising Process

1. **The Teaser:** Send a short email with 3 bullet points about traction and your Pitch Deck PDF.
2. **The First Meeting (30 mins):** Share your story, the problem, and show them the working app.
3. **Due Diligence:** If they like it, they will ask for your financial model and legal documents.
4. **Term Sheet:** They give you an offer outlining the money and equity.
5. **Money in the Bank:** You sign, and the funds are wired to the TasteNova account.

---

<!-- Slide 12 -->
# Final Golden Rules

- **Raise slightly more than you need.** Things always take longer and cost more than expected.
- **Don't wait to be "perfect".** Your app is built, you are ready to start having conversations now.
- **Create FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out).** Talk to multiple investors at the same time so no one feels they have all the leverage.
